Problem

Claims operators depended on Google Sheets, Google Chat, SOPs and practice-specific information sources to find critical workflow updates. Finding the right information could depend on knowing where it was stored or who to ask.

What I explored

I mapped the existing information-retrieval workflow and defined a centralized Operations Hub to reduce context switching and improve access to operational knowledge. I worked from real RCM workflows and practice-specific exceptions, not a generic knowledge tool.

Prototype included

  • Practice Directory
  • Practice Updates / Holds
  • SOPs & Guidelines
  • Login / Portal information
  • Credential information
  • Search and operational knowledge access
  • Notifications / watchlist and admin controls

My role

Problem identification → Workflow mapping → PRD → Prototype → Iteration

Product lesson

An internal AI assistant is not simply a chatbot. Its usefulness depends on the quality, context, source and accessibility of operational knowledge. I identified AI-assisted retrieval as a possible capability, with source attribution, permissions and update ownership as core considerations.

Context

U.S. dental RCM work depends on practice-specific rules, payer requirements and frequently changing information.

What I built

A PRD covering user needs, workflows, functional requirements and MVP scope, plus a functional prototype with the modules above.

What I learned

Knowledge quality and source clarity matter more than the assistant interface.

What's next

Working toward user validation: measuring information lookup time, search success, dependency on teammates and how easily operational updates are found.

The question is not only whether someone completed training. It is whether there is enough evidence that they are ready to do the work.

Currently building

4C

The problem

Students, career changers and early professionals can learn skills, build projects and contribute to work, but proving their actual capabilities can still be difficult.

A resume mainly communicates claims. LinkedIn communicates professional identity. GitHub provides evidence primarily for code. Portfolios show selected work. 4C explores whether collaboration, contribution and proof can become part of a connected professional identity.

The current idea

FindTeamBuildContributeProveDiscover

Potential experiences: finding collaborators, creating or joining teams and projects, working together, recording meaningful contribution, showing progress, building evidence, showcasing work publicly and being discovered by recruiters, founders and companies.

What I've done so far

OnboardingPeople discoveryTeam formationProject workspaceCollaborationProject showcase

The MVP journey I've designed.

  • Researched the ecosystem and competitors to find gaps in how students and early-career builders find collaborators, form teams and gain real project experience
  • Did user and problem discovery across students, builders, project owners, startups and recruiters to evaluate existing solutions
  • Iterated the concept based on feasibility, trust, marketplace and competitive constraints
  • Built interactive AI-assisted prototypes to test flows, communicate the concept and gather early feedback
  • Explored business models, GTM strategy, partnerships with software-training institutes and an early pilot model for testing team formation with real students

How I Think About Products

Start with the problem

Understand the actual workflow before deciding what to build.

Make it tangible

Use prototypes to turn an abstract idea into something people can react to.

Question assumptions

Research competitors, users and existing alternatives instead of assuming the first idea is right.

Iterate

Change the product when evidence shows the original assumption is weak.
